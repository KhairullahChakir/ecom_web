4. Key Takeaways

The "SOTA" Winner: TabM

While Random Forest barely edged out TabM in raw AUC, TabM is the superior choice for production because: 1. Recall Sensitivity: TabM achieves a much higher recall (0.832 vs 0.664), meaning it identifies 83% of actual buyers. In e-commerce, missing a buyer is more expensive than a false alarm. 2. GPU Scalability: As the dataset grows, TabM (PyTorch) will scale better than Random Forest (which is CPU-bound). 3. Optimizability: As seen in Phase 3/4, TabM can be pruned and exported to ONNX for 0.11ms latency.

The Failure of Transformers

FT-Transformer performed poorly (0.772 AUC). This is a known phenomenon in tabular ML: Transformers often require much larger datasets (100k+ rows) to beat specialized MLP ensembles like TabM on smaller datasets (~12k rows).

5. Final Selection

The TabM (ONNX) model was selected for the backend/ microservice. It represents the "Sweet Spot" on the Pruning Efficiency Frontier—offering high recall, competitive AUC, and industrial-grade inference speed.

6. Verification

All models were tested on a dedicated 10% holdout set (random_state=42) with standardized preprocessing (StandardScaler + Label Encoding) to ensure zero data leakage.
